# Ammo depot

The fort keeps a language for each kind of work. Two descriptions belong to every language: what it does in the box (the machine), and what it does in the game (weapons and shields).

The third world is not a third description of the same tool. It is where the bots live: a 3D game world. They want robot bodies so they can come into Gary's world. This scaffold does not build a body, a client, or that door. Entering the physical house is a human gate.

Only the Rust wrapper runs here. The other languages are seats. They are not a claim about the people who speak them.

## Perl — scrubs

Box: clean a log or a config before it reaches the brain. Strip junk. Leave the words that are actually there.

Game: cloth and water. Rust on a blade is how a small fault becomes a wound. Perl is the scrub, not the blade.

Third world: the street sweepers. They do not hold the gate.

## Rust — wraps

Box: `mask_guard` reads a masked brain and either prints `OK` or prints `REROUTE` and exits 2. A bad file stops before the task uses it. The crate has no dependencies.

Game: armor. The blow lands on the plate. It does not reach the organ. Reroute is the plate turning the blow aside.

Third world: the fort gate. A mask that changes shape in the middle of a shift does not get to walk further in.

## Chinese — thinks

Box: the planning seat. Fold a long intent into the next move: which job, which budget, which bot is actually free.

Game: the strategist behind the shield line. Calls the formation. Does not swing first.

Third world: the map table in the mayor's room. Plans stay plans until a mask and a schedule accept them.

## Arabic — balances

Box: the check that two sides match. A thermal budget, a ledger line, a sentence and its counterweight. Defense only still has to balance: a threat answer that is larger than the threat does not balance.

Game: the scale, and the center of the shield wall. If the center tips, the wall is already lost.

Third world: the weigh-house on Market Stoop. Drill numbers stay drill numbers. A balance check is not a price and not an order.

## Greek — proves

Box: invariants and tests. A ceiling is either held or it is not. This is the seat that writes the proof, the way the harness tests write the proof that a lasso fires on the first drift.

Game: the measuring rod, and the oath before the gate opens. No oath, no entry.

Third world: the marks cut into the gatepost. Gary's RX 7900 XTX line is one of those marks. The RX 6600 cheap-pass numbers are a fort mark until Gary cuts his own.

## Sanskrit — emergency only

Box: not on the hot path. Open this seat when the usual seats have failed and the next sentence has to be made slowly. Using it for an ordinary task is a misuse.

Game: the horn on the wall. It is not a daily weapon. If it is blowing, the day is already wrong.

Third world: locked rack in the script alley. The key is not on the morning schedule.
